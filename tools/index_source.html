Probabilistically Robust Tracking of Language-Conditioned Oracle Motion Plans by Black-Box Imitation Learners

Paper (Coming soon)

Abstract

Ensuring formal, robust safety guarantees for black-box foundation models used as motion planners remains an open challenge. Existing approaches commonly rely on assumptions about the model or the nature of the disturbance, or certify safety through the training process, which rules out using large, off-the-shelf models. We introduce Probabilistically Robust Tracking of Language-Conditioned Experts, which combines conformal prediction and contraction theory to certify incremental exponential boundedness of the unknown true dynamics around a semantically aware, human-aligned expert. A transformer is trained to imitate these human-aligned expert motion plans, while a closed-loop contracting controller tracks the transformer's predicted path. Split conformal calibration of a closed-loop contraction-violation score produces a tube around the expert with finite-sample coverage. The expert is not required online, and the contraction dynamics preserve the guarantee under shifts in the start distribution. We further show that these guarantees extend to perception: in real-world settings where the sources of uncertainty are unknown, calibration against an expert built from the true environment yields perception-aware tubes. In simulation, the tubes achieve empirical coverage above the target level across all route preferences and under start-distribution shift, and we demonstrate perception-aware tubes on the Starling 2 vision-based drone platform.

Problem formulation

\(L_1\) is the goal sentence, for example “fly to the far side of the chair.” \(L_2\) is the route preference, for example “give every obstacle the widest practical berth.”

  • \(x_d(L_1,L_2,t)\), \(u_d(t)\): dynamically feasible language-conditioned oracle trajectory and input, available only offline.
  • \(\hat x_d(L_1,L_2,t)\), \(\hat u_d(t)\): dynamically feasible transformer trajectory and input, available online.
  • \(x(t)\), \(u(t)\): measured closed-loop state and applied control.
  • \(M(x)\): control contraction metric; \(D(t)=d_M(x_d(t),x(t))\): oracle-relative Riemannian distance.

The same minimum-control QP converts oracle and transformer waypoints into \((x_d,u_d)\) and \((\hat x_d,\hat u_d)\).

Contracting controller

\[ u=\hat u_d+k,\qquad k=\begin{cases}0,&a_0\le 0,\\[2pt]-\dfrac{a_0}{\|b\|^2}b,&a_0>0,\end{cases} \]

The geodesic terms \(a_0\) and \(b\) evaluate the CCM condition between \(\hat x_d\) and the measured state \(x\). The correction \(k\) is the minimum-norm input needed to enforce \(\dot E\le-2\lambda E\), where \(E=d_M(\hat x_d,x)^2\). The oracle is never used online.

Score definition and certified tube

\[ \rho(t)=\big[\dot D(t)+\lambda D(t)\big]_+,\qquad S=\max\!\left(\sup_t\rho(t),\;\beta\frac{\lambda}{\sqrt w}L\right). \]

\(\rho\) is the violation of the contraction condition. \(L\) is zero when the transformer preserves the oracle corridor and positive when it does not. Split conformal calibration returns quantile \(q\), giving

\[ D(t)\le e^{-\lambda t}D(0)+\frac q\lambda\big(1-e^{-\lambda t}\big),\qquad \|p(t)-p_d(t)\|\le\sqrt w\,D(t) \]

with trajectory-level probability at least \(1-\alpha\).

Simulation dynamics and disturbances

\[ x=(p_x,p_y,\theta,v),\quad u=(\omega,a),\quad \dot x=f(x)+Bu+\zeta(x,u), \]

\[ f(x)=\begin{bmatrix}v\cos\theta\\v\sin\theta\\0\\0\end{bmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\0&0\\1&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}. \]

\(\zeta(x,u)\) is an unmodelled disturbance. The study evaluates Gaussian, heavy-tailed, and state-dependent drag/slip regimes, plus a shifted start distribution; the certificate does not assume any of these disturbance models.

Perception Uncertainty-Aware Tube

Calibration compares a transformer plan built from the perceived environment \(\hat E\) with an oracle built from the Vicon-ground-truth environment \(E\), absorbing the combined planner and perception mismatch without a sensor-error model.

Framework: perception, language-conditioned planning, transformer imitation, ideal double-integrator tracking, and split-conformal tube calibration.

Problem formulation

\(E\) is the Vicon-ground-truth environment and \(\hat E\) the onboard perceived environment. \(L_1\) specifies the goal, for example “fly to the person,” and \(L_2\) specifies the route preference, for example “give the person extra space.”

\[ x_d=x_d(E,L_1,L_2,t),\qquad \hat x_d=\hat x_d(\hat E,L_1,L_2,t). \]

The offline oracle uses \(E\); the onboard transformer uses \(\hat E\). The resulting difference includes planning and perception uncertainty.

Controller

\[ u=\hat u_d-K\hat e=\hat u_d-K(x-\hat x_d),\qquad \hat e=x-\hat x_d. \]

With \(e=x-x_d\), the oracle-relative error dynamics are

\[ \begin{aligned} \dot e &= A_c e + R, \\ A_c &= A-BK, \\ R &= B\!\left[\hat u_d-u_d+K(\hat x_d-x_d)\right]. \end{aligned} \]

The gain \(K\) makes \(A_c\) contracting. A contraction matrix \(P\succ0\) satisfies

\[ A_c^{\top}P+PA_c\preceq-2\lambda_P P, \qquad \lambda_P>0. \]

Score and safety tube

\[ S=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|P^{1/2}R(t)\|_2,\qquad W(t)=\|P^{1/2}(x(t)-x_d(t))\|_2. \]

The score is the largest weighted closed-loop residual. With conformal quantile \(q\),

\[ \|x(t)-x_d(t)\|_2\le\frac{W(0)e^{-\lambda_Pt}+\frac q{\lambda_P}(1-e^{-\lambda_Pt})}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(P)}} \]

with probability at least \(1-\alpha\). This is the final probabilistically robust conformal contraction tube between the transformer-driven trajectory and the offline oracle.

Dynamics and tracking assumption

\[ x=(p,\dot p),\qquad u=\ddot p,\qquad \dot x=Ax+Bu,\quad A=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\I\end{bmatrix}. \]

Because the hardware data do not include a separate trajectory rollout for the oracle, the analysis assumes the fast cascaded PID loops in PX4 ideally track the transformer’s dynamically feasible double-integrator reference. Under this assumption, the calibrated residual captures the transformer and perception mismatch relative to the offline oracle.
