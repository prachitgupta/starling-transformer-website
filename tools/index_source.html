Probabilistically Robust and Safe Tracking of Language-Conditioned Natural-Language Tasks

Paper

Abstract

A transformer plans waypoints from a scene and two natural-language inputs: a goal sentence L1 and a route preference L2. The vehicle flies the transformer plan under a contracting controller that feeds back only the measured state. Offline, a language-conditioned expert (HRRT* with a GPT route selector) gives the trajectory the vehicle should have followed. The certificate bounds the Riemannian distance D(t) from the expert to the vehicle by D(t) ≤ e−λtD(0) + (q/λ)(1 − e−λt) with probability at least 1 − α, where q is the split-conformal quantile of a single conditional score built on the contraction residual [Ḍ + λD]+ with a small penalty when the language preference is deviated. No model of the disturbance, the tracking error, or the planner's error is assumed. On 2,598 held-out scenes with the trained transformer and all six preference types, under three disturbance regimes, the tube covers 95–96% of test episodes against a 90% target with a mean position radius of 0.77–0.81 m, and keeps its coverage under a start-distribution shift where constructions without contraction drop to 61–87%.

Framework

Contraction of the true dynamics to a language-conditioned expert through the transformer approximation of the expert. The expert is not available online.

Simulation Results

Disturbance ablation studies for a standard 4D Dubins car with unmodelled disturbance. Trained transformer, HRRT* expert with the GPT selector, all six language preferences, 1,000 scene-level splits with 200 calibration and 200 test episodes each, target coverage 90%.

Problem formulation

Standard 4D Dubins car with unmodelled disturbance \(\zeta\), state \(x=(p_x,p_y,\theta,v)\) and input \(u=(\omega,a)\):

\[ \dot x = f(x) + B u + \zeta(x,u). \]

Three trajectories:

  • \(x_d(L_1,L_2,t)\): language-conditioned expert. HRRT* proposes a family of safe routes and, based on the human preference, one route is selected. Offline only.
  • \(\hat x_d(L_1,L_2,t)\): transformer approximation of the expert. Flown online.
  • \(x(t)\): true dynamics under the contracting controller.

The same QP solver pipeline turns the transformer waypoints and the expert route into dynamically feasible references \((\hat x_d,\hat u_d)\) and \((x_d,u_d)\).

Contracting controller

An SOS control contraction metric \(M(x)\) with rate \(\lambda\) gives the minimum-norm controller, which feeds back only the measured state and never uses the expert:

\[ u = \hat u_d + k,\qquad k = \begin{cases} 0, & a_0 \le 0,\\[2pt] -\dfrac{a_0}{\|b\|^2}\,b, & a_0 > 0, \end{cases} \]

where \(a_0\) and \(b\) are the contraction condition evaluated along the geodesic from \(\hat x_d\) to \(x\) [8].

Score definition for the conformal tube

With \(D(t)=d_M(x_d(t),x(t))\) the Riemannian distance from the expert to the vehicle, the score quantifies the difference of the transformer's predictions from the language-conditioned expert. We use a conditional score with a small penalty \(L\) if the language preference is deviated:

\[ \rho(t) = \big[\dot D(t) + \lambda D(t)\big]_+,\qquad S = \max\Big(\sup_t \rho(t),\; \beta\,\tfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{w}}\,L\Big). \]

Split conformal prediction [9] on \(n\) calibration episodes gives \(q\), the \(\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil\)-th smallest score. With probability at least \(1-\alpha\), with no assumption on the disturbance,

\[ D(t) \le e^{-\lambda t} D(0) + \frac{q}{\lambda}\big(1-e^{-\lambda t}\big), \qquad \|p(t)-p_d(t)\| \le \sqrt{w}\,D(t). \]

Hardware Implementation with Environmental Uncertainties

We tighten the safe space and incorporate the effect of perception uncertainty in our probabilistically robust conformal tubes without any assumptions on the sensor model or the nature of disturbance. Split conformal prediction needs exchangeability, so the workspace is restricted to regions with 100% detectability. Training data is synthetic; calibration data is sampled from the same distribution with the perceived obstacle coming from the actual drone.

Hardware problem formulation

Ideal tracking by the PX4 cascaded PID gives a planar double integrator

\[ \dot x = Ax + Bu,\qquad A=\begin{bmatrix}0&I\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\quad B=\begin{bmatrix}0\\I\end{bmatrix}. \]

\(L_1\) is the goal sentence and \(L_2\) the route preference. \(E\) is the known environment from Vicon ground truth and \(\hat E\) the perceived environment.

\[ x_d = x_d(E,L_1,L_2,t),\qquad \hat x_d = \hat x_d(\hat E,L_1,L_2,t), \]

\[ e = x - x_d\ \text{(offline)},\qquad \hat e = x - \hat x_d\ \text{(online)},\qquad u = \hat u_d - K\hat e. \]

\[ \dot e = A_c e + R,\quad A_c = A-BK,\quad R = B\big[\underbrace{\hat u_d-u_d}_{\text{planner}} + \underbrace{K(\hat x_d-x_d)}_{\text{perception}}\big]. \]

With \(A_c^\top P + PA_c \preceq -2\lambda_P P\), the score is \(S=\sup_t \|P^{1/2}R(t)\|_2\) and, with \(W(t)=\|P^{1/2}(x-x_d)\|_2\),

\[ \|x(t)-x_d(t)\|_2 \le \frac{W(0)e^{-\lambda_P t} + \frac{q}{\lambda_P}\big(1-e^{-\lambda_P t}\big)}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(P)}}. \]

The perception pipeline is YOLOv8-based object detection integrated with point-cloud estimates from ToF sensors.
